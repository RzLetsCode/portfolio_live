Advanced & Tricky Matrix Inverses

Conceptual challenges focusing on symmetry, algebraic shortcuts, and structural traps.

Question 1: The Self-Inverse Reflection Matrix

Find the inverse of matrix \( A \).

$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix} $$

The Trick: This represents a reflection across the x-axis. Applying it twice brings you right back to where you started ($A^2 = I$).
Step 1: Determinant
\( (1 \cdot -1) - (0 \cdot 0) = -1 \)
Step 2: Adjugate & Scale
Swap main diagonal elements, negate off-diagonals, and divide by determinant \(-1\).
Final Inverse: $$ A^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix} $$ Fun Fact: Any involution matrix is its own inverse ($A^{-1} = A$).
Question 2: The 90-Degree Rotation Matrix

Find the inverse of matrix \( B \).

$$ B = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

The Trick: This matrix rotates vectors counter-clockwise by \(90^\circ\). To undo it, you must rotate clockwise by \(90^\circ\).
Step 1: Determinant
\( (0 \cdot 0) - (-1 \cdot 1) = 0 - (-1) = 1 \)
Step 2: Apply Formula
Since det is 1, swap main diagonal (\(0\) and \(0\)) and negate off-diagonals (\(-1 \to 1\), \(1 \to -1\)).
Final Inverse: $$ B^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$
Question 3: The Scaled Identity Matrix

Find the inverse of matrix \( C \).

$$ C = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 \end{bmatrix} $$

The Trick: This is a scalar multiple of the identity matrix (\(4I\)). You don't need cofactors; just invert the scalar multiplier!
The Shortcut:
For any scalar \(k\) multiplied by an identity matrix of size \(n\), the inverse is simply \(\frac{1}{k}I\).
Final Inverse: $$ C^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{4} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{1}{4} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \frac{1}{4} \end{bmatrix} $$
Question 4: The Proportional Rows Trap

Find the inverse of matrix \( D \).

$$ D = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 6 \\ 2 & 4 \end{bmatrix} $$

The Trick: Look closely at the rows. Row 2 is exactly \(\frac{2}{3}\) of Row 1. Linearly dependent rows mean the matrix collapses dimensions.
Step 1: Determinant
\( (3 \cdot 4) - (6 \cdot 2) = 12 - 12 = 0 \)
Final Answer:
Because the determinant is \(0\), Matrix \(D\) is singular and has no inverse.
Question 5: The Unipotent Upper Triangular 3x3

Find the inverse of matrix \( E \).

$$ E = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

The Trick: A matrix with ones on the main diagonal and zeros below it has a determinant of exactly \(1\). Its inverse maintains the upper-triangular pattern with negated shift values.
Step 1: Determinant
Product of main diagonal = \(1 \times 1 \times 1 = 1\).
Step 2: Elimination / Formula
Applying row operations or cofactor expansion yields the clean upper-triangular inverse.
Final Inverse: $$ E^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -2 & 5 \\ 0 & 1 & -4 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
Question 6: The Determinant-1 Integer Matrix

Find the inverse of matrix \( F \).

$$ F = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 3 \\ 3 & 2 \end{bmatrix} $$

The Trick: When the determinant of a 2x2 integer matrix equals \(1\), the inverse is literally just the adjugate matrix with no fractional scalar division!
Step 1: Determinant
\( (5 \cdot 2) - (3 \cdot 3) = 10 - 9 = 1 \)
Step 2: Rearrange
Swap main diagonal (\(5 \leftrightarrow 2\)), negate off-diagonals (\(3 \to -3\)).
Final Inverse: $$ F^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -3 \\ -3 & 5 \end{bmatrix} $$
Question 7: The Anti-Diagonal Exchange Matrix

Find the inverse of matrix \( G \).

$$ G = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

The Trick: This matrix reverses the order of vector components. Applying it twice returns the original vector (\(G^2 = I\)).
The Shortcut:
Because swapping the first and last row/column twice gets you back to identity, the matrix is its own inverse: \(G^{-1} = G\).
Final Inverse: $$ G^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$
Question 8: The Lower Triangular 3x3

Find the inverse of matrix \( H \).

$$ H = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 4 & 0 \\ 3 & 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$

The Trick: The inverse of a lower triangular matrix is always lower triangular. Use forward substitution or cofactor evaluation.
Step 1: Determinant
Product of diagonal: \(2 \times 4 \times 1 = 8\) (Invertible).
Step 2: Cofactors / Inversion
Computing minors and dividing by \(\det(H) = 8\).
Final Inverse: $$ H^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{2} & 0 & 0 \\ -\frac{1}{8} & \frac{1}{4} & 0 \\ \frac{1}{4} & -\frac{1}{2} & 1 \end{bmatrix} $$
Question 9: The Duplicate Rows Trap

Find the inverse of matrix \( J \).

$$ J = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix} $$

The Trick: Notice that Row 1 and Row 3 are identical. Whenever two rows or columns in a matrix match completely, the determinant is zero.
Step 1: Determinant Check
Evaluating along any expansion or recognizing linear dependence yields \(\det(J) = 0\).
Final Answer:
Because the determinant is \(0\), Matrix \(J\) is singular and has no inverse.
Question 10: The Fractional Diagonal 3x3

Find the inverse of matrix \( K \).

$$ K = \begin{bmatrix} -\frac{1}{2} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{1}{3} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -4 \end{bmatrix} $$

The Trick: Applying the diagonal matrix shortcut with fractions and negative signs—just take the reciprocal of each diagonal entry (\(\frac{1}{x}\)).
The Shortcut:
  • Reciprocal of \(-\frac{1}{2}\) is \(-2\)
  • Reciprocal of \(\frac{1}{3}\) is \(3\)
  • Reciprocal of \(-4\) is \(-\frac{1}{4}\)
Final Inverse: $$ K^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} -2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -\frac{1}{4} \end{bmatrix} $$
