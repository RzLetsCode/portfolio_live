Matrix Inverse Under Change of Basis

How a transformation and its inverse change under a new coordinate system

1. The Mathematical Principle

If a matrix \( A \) represents a linear transformation in the standard basis, changing to a new basis via matrix \( P \) transforms \( A \) into matrix \( B \):

\[ B = P^{-1}AP \]

What happens to the inverse matrix \( A^{-1} \)? Does it also undergo a similarity transformation? Yes!

Derivation for \( B^{-1} \):

Taking the inverse of both sides of \( B = P^{-1}AP \):

\[ B^{-1} = (P^{-1} A P)^{-1} \]

Applying the reverse order rule for matrix inverses, \( (XYZ)^{-1} = Z^{-1} Y^{-1} X^{-1} \):

\[ B^{-1} = P^{-1} A^{-1} (P^{-1})^{-1} \]

Since \( (P^{-1})^{-1} = P \), we get:

\[ \mathbf{B^{-1} = P^{-1} A^{-1} P} \quad \blacksquare \]

Key Insight: Just as \( B \) is the matrix \( A \) expressed in the new basis, \( B^{-1} \) is simply the inverse matrix \( A^{-1} \) expressed in that same new basis using the same transition matrix \( P \)!
