Tricky Matrix Inverses

Test your conceptual understanding of 2x2 and 3x3 matrices. These questions are designed to trick you if you rely only on brute-force calculation!

Question 1: The Algebraic 2x2

Find the inverse of matrix \( A \). Under what condition does this inverse not exist?

$$ A = \begin{bmatrix} x & 2 \\ 3 & x \end{bmatrix} $$

The Trick: Treat the variable \( x \) exactly the same way you handle normal numbers.
Step 1: Determinant
\( (x \cdot x) - (2 \cdot 3) = x^2 - 6 \)
Step 2: Rearrange
Swap main diagonal, flip signs of the other: \( \begin{bmatrix} x & -2 \\ -3 & x \end{bmatrix} \)
Final Inverse: $$ A^{-1} = \frac{1}{x^2 - 6} \begin{bmatrix} x & -2 \\ -3 & x \end{bmatrix} $$ When does it not exist?
An inverse does not exist if the determinant is 0. If \( x^2 - 6 = 0 \), the matrix is singular. Therefore, it does not exist when \( x = \sqrt{6} \) or \( x = -\sqrt{6} \).
Question 2: The Trigonometric 2x2

Find the inverse of the standard 2D rotation matrix \( B \).

$$ B = \begin{bmatrix} \cos(\theta) & -\sin(\theta) \\ \sin(\theta) & \cos(\theta) \end{bmatrix} $$

The Trick: Recognizing fundamental trigonometric identities.
Step 1: Determinant
\( (\cos\theta \cdot \cos\theta) - (-\sin\theta \cdot \sin\theta) = \cos^2(\theta) + \sin^2(\theta) \)
In trigonometry, this identity always equals exactly 1.
Step 2: Rearrange
\( \begin{bmatrix} \cos(\theta) & \sin(\theta) \\ -\sin(\theta) & \cos(\theta) \end{bmatrix} \)
Final Inverse: $$ B^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos(\theta) & \sin(\theta) \\ -\sin(\theta) & \cos(\theta) \end{bmatrix} $$ Fun Fact: For a rotation matrix, the inverse is simply its transpose!
Question 3: The Sequential 3x3

Find the inverse of matrix \( C \).

$$ C = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 9 \end{bmatrix} $$

The Trick: Check the determinant before doing the massive 3x3 Adjugate calculations!
Calculating the Determinant:
\( \text{Det} = 1(5 \cdot 9 - 6 \cdot 8) - 2(4 \cdot 9 - 6 \cdot 7) + 3(4 \cdot 8 - 5 \cdot 7) \)
\( \text{Det} = 1(45 - 48) - 2(36 - 42) + 3(32 - 35) \)
\( \text{Det} = 1(-3) - 2(-6) + 3(-3) \)
\( \text{Det} = -3 + 12 - 9 = 0 \)
Final Answer:
Because the determinant is exactly 0, Matrix \( C \) is a singular matrix and has no inverse.
(Rule of thumb: Any 3x3 matrix where the numbers count up sequentially will always have a determinant of 0).
Question 4: The "Scary" Diagonal 3x3

Find the inverse of matrix \( D \).

$$ D = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 5 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix} $$

The Trick: Recognizing diagonal matrices. You do not need to calculate 9 separate minors and cofactors for this!
The Shortcut:
For a Diagonal Matrix (all numbers are 0 except the main diagonal), the shortcut is simply taking the reciprocal of each number on the main diagonal.
Final Inverse: $$ D^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{2} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{1}{5} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix} $$
