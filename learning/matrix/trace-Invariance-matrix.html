Basis Independence & Trace Invariance

Mathematical Derivation & 3D Interactive Demonstration

1. Mathematical Proof & Assumptions

Key Assumptions:
  • Linearity: The transformation \( T: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n \) is linear.
  • Invertibility of Basis Change: Matrix \( P \), which translates vectors from the new basis back to the standard basis, is square and invertible (\( \det(P) \neq 0 \)).
  • Finite Dimensions: Operating within finite-dimensional vector spaces (e.g., 2D or 3D).

Algebraic Derivation

When changing your basis using a transition matrix \( P \), the relationship between matrix \( A \) (old basis) and matrix \( B \) (new basis) is given by the Similarity Transformation:

\[ B = P^{-1}AP \]

To prove that \( \text{Tr}(A) = \text{Tr}(B) \), we use the Cyclic Property of the Trace, which states that for any two compatible matrices \( X \) and \( Y \):

\[ \text{Tr}(XY) = \text{Tr}(YX) \]

Substitute \( X = P^{-1}A \) and \( Y = P \):

\[ \text{Tr}(B) = \text{Tr}(P^{-1}AP) \]

Apply the cyclic property by shifting \( P \) to the front:

\[ \text{Tr}(B) = \text{Tr}\big(P \cdot (P^{-1}A)\big) \]

Using matrix associativity:

\[ \text{Tr}(B) = \text{Tr}\big((P P^{-1}) A\big) \]

Since \( P P^{-1} = I \) (the identity matrix):

\[ \text{Tr}(B) = \text{Tr}(I \cdot A) = \text{Tr}(A) \] \[ \mathbf{\text{Tr}(B) = \text{Tr}(A)} \quad \blacksquare \]

2. Step-by-Step Calculation Example

Here is the exact numeric breakdown of the 3D transformation visualized below:

Step A: Matrix \( A \)

Transformation in Standard Basis:

\[ A = \begin{pmatrix} 3.0 & 1.0 & 0.0 \\ 0.0 & 2.0 & 1.0 \\ 0.0 & 0.0 & 1.5 \end{pmatrix} \]
Tr(A) = 3.0 + 2.0 + 1.5 = 6.5

Step B: Basis Matrix \( P \)

Skewed Basis & Inverse \( P^{-1} \):

\[ P \approx \begin{pmatrix} 1.2 & 0.3 & -0.2 \\ 0.1 & 1.1 & 0.4 \\ 0.2 & -0.1 & 0.9 \end{pmatrix} \] \[ P^{-1} \approx \begin{pmatrix} 0.846 & -0.205 & 0.288 \\ -0.008 & 0.920 & -0.411 \\ -0.189 & 0.148 & 1.060 \end{pmatrix} \]

Step C: Compute \( B = P^{-1}AP \)

First compute \( AP \):

\[ AP = \begin{pmatrix} 3.7 & 2.0 & -0.2 \\ 0.2 & 2.1 & 1.7 \\ 0.3 & -0.15 & 1.35 \end{pmatrix} \]

Then compute \( P^{-1}(AP) \):

\[ B = \begin{pmatrix} 3.17 & 1.22 & -0.13 \\ 0.03 & 1.97 & 1.01 \\ -0.35 & -0.22 & 1.36 \end{pmatrix} \]

Step D: Calculate \( \text{Tr}(B) \)

Summing the diagonal elements of \( B \):

\[ \text{Tr}(B) = B_{11} + B_{22} + B_{33} \] \[ \text{Tr}(B) = 3.17 + 1.97 + 1.36 \]
Tr(B) = 6.5
