Vector Calculus & Linear Algebra

Scalar Projection of Vectors

A Comprehensive Guide: Intuition, Formulas, and Worked Examples

1 Intuition: The "Shadow" Concept

The scalar projection of vector \(\mathbf{x}\) onto vector \(\mathbf{y}\) (written as \(\text{comp}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{x}\)) measures the effective length of vector \(\mathbf{x}\) along the direction of vector \(\mathbf{y}\).

💡 The Flashlight Analogy

Imagine vector \(\mathbf{y}\) is a straight road, and vector \(\mathbf{x}\) is a stick tilted upward from the start of the road. If you hold a flashlight high above, shining perpendicular to the road, the length of the shadow cast by the stick along the road is the scalar projection.

2 Mathematical Formulas

Depending on what information you are given, there are two standard ways to calculate scalar projection:

Trigonometric Form
$$\text{comp}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{x} = \|\mathbf{x}\| \cos\theta$$

Used when you know the magnitude of \(\mathbf{x}\) and the angle \(\theta\) between vectors \(\mathbf{x}\) and \(\mathbf{y}\).

Component / Dot Product Form
$$\text{comp}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{x} = \frac{\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{y}}{\|\mathbf{y}\|}$$

Used when vectors are given in component form \(\langle x_1, x_2, x_3 \rangle\). No angle required!

3 Sign Interpretation (Angle Behavior)

The sign of the scalar projection indicates the relative direction of the two vectors:

Angle (\(\theta\)) Alignment Scalar Projection Sign Meaning
\(\theta < 90^\circ\) Acute Angle Positive (\(> 0\)) Shadow points forward in the same direction as \(\mathbf{y}\).
\(\theta = 90^\circ\) Perpendicular Zero (\(= 0\)) Light is directly overhead; no shadow along \(\mathbf{y}\).
\(\theta > 90^\circ\) Obtuse Angle Negative (\(< 0\)) Shadow points backward in the opposite direction of \(\mathbf{y}\).

4 Step-by-Step Worked Example (Evaluating at \(k = 0\))

Problem Statement:

Given the parametric vectors \(\mathbf{x}(k) = \langle 3 + k, \, 4, \, -1 \rangle\) and \(\mathbf{y}(k) = \langle 2, \, 1 + k, \, 2 \rangle\), calculate the scalar projection of \(\mathbf{x}\) onto \(\mathbf{y}\) when \(k = 0\).

Step 1: Evaluate the Vectors at \(k = 0\)

Substitute \(k = 0\) directly into both vector expressions to get simple numeric vectors:

\(\mathbf{x} = \langle 3 + 0, \, 4, \, -1 \rangle = \langle 3, \, 4, \, -1 \rangle\)
\(\mathbf{y} = \langle 2, \, 1 + 0, \, 2 \rangle = \langle 2, \, 1, \, 2 \rangle\)

Step 2: Compute the Dot Product \(\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{y}\)

Multiply corresponding components and add them together:

\(\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{y} = (3)(2) + (4)(1) + (-1)(2)\)
\(\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{y} = 6 + 4 - 2 = \mathbf{8}\)

Step 3: Compute the Magnitude of the Baseline Vector \(\|\mathbf{y}\|\)

Calculate the length of the vector being projected onto (\(\mathbf{y}\)):

\(\|\mathbf{y}\| = \sqrt{2^2 + 1^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{4 + 1 + 4} = \sqrt{9} = \mathbf{3}\)

Step 4: Apply the Scalar Projection Formula

Divide the dot product by the magnitude of \(\mathbf{y}\):

$$\text{comp}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{x} = \frac{\mathbf{x} \cdot \mathbf{y}}{\|\mathbf{y}\|} = \frac{8}{3} \approx 2.67$$

📌 Important Student Pitfalls & Key Takeaways

  • Which magnitude goes in the denominator? Always place the magnitude of the target vector (\(\mathbf{y}\) in \(\text{comp}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{x}\)) in the denominator, because \(\mathbf{y}\) serves as the baseline scale.
  • Scalar vs. Vector Projection: Scalar projection produces a single number (scalar). Vector projection turns that number back into a vector pointing along \(\mathbf{y}\).
  • Order Matters in Notation: \(\text{comp}_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{x}\) means projecting \(\mathbf{x}\) ONTO \(\mathbf{y}\).
